Case history

A 45-year-old man presents to the chronic pain clinic with a 2-year history of debilitating pain in all four limbs.

He explains that the pain started after an industrial accident in which he inhaled pure ethanol fumes. He needed immediate hospital admission and a short stay in intensive care.

He has constant electric, tingling, unpleasant sensations in all his limbs. In his legs the painful sensations extend from the mid-shin to the foot, and sensation is dull as far as the mid-thigh. In his hands the fingertips are mainly affected, with a buzzing dysaesthesia that stops him holding objects and affects his grip.

He has been seen by the neurology team. A nerve conduction study was carried out and was normal.

He has well-controlled hypertension and type 1 diabetes. His diabetic control was poor before the accident, but he is now compliant with a good diet and his medication.

He has been through a long legal process with his employer and successfully obtained compensation for the accident. He is disappointed, as he feels the amount is very small compared with the disruption to his life.

He lives with his wife and two children (aged 18 and 14). He is currently unemployed, as he struggles to use his hands and cannot tolerate footwear for any length of time. Even simple tasks such as holding a mug or eating with a spoon are difficult.

His mood is very low and he is extremely frustrated: he used to be a very active person and is now mostly housebound. His social life has become non-existent, because he feels shame at being around other people and guilt that they have to modify their activities for him to join in. His sleep is usually interrupted when blankets or sheets trigger his foot pain.
